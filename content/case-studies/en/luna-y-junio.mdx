## The context

Luna y Junio is a Peruvian second-hand clothing shop that sells one-of-a-kind garments through weekly launches, or *drops*. Each post can attract several interested buyers at the same time, even though only one item is available.

When sales are managed through comments, private messages and payment receipts sent across different channels, the operation becomes difficult to control. The team has to determine who reserved first, confirm payments, respond to everyone else and coordinate every delivery manually.

As the audience grew, so did the operational workload.

## The challenge

The brand needed to preserve the personal feel and excitement of its launches while introducing a fairer, more orderly process.

The solution needed to:

- Prevent the same garment from being reserved by several people.
- Give priority transparently to the person who arrived first.
- Recover sales opportunities when a reservation was not completed.
- Centralise receipts from Yape, Plin and other payment methods.
- Make sales and delivery tracking easier.
- Retain a distinct visual identity instead of feeling like a generic online store.

## My responsibility

My responsibility covered the platform’s design and development: defining the purchasing flow, timed reservations and waiting list; organising receipts, payments and deliveries; and building the administration panel used to manage *drops* and garments.

## The solution

I designed and built a bespoke commerce platform that connects the buying experience with the business’s internal operation.

Customers can discover the current *drop*, see when the next launch is coming, review each garment and reserve it when it becomes available. The reservation is held for a limited period while payment is completed. If it is not completed, the system releases the garment and offers it to the next person on the waiting list.

The platform also includes:

- Publishing and scheduling for *drops*.
- Individual garment releases.
- Timed reservations without duplicate bookings.
- An automatic waiting list.
- Private receipt submission and review.
- A reservation history for every customer.
- Payment and delivery management from an administration panel.
- An offers section for garments from previous collections.
- Operational data exports.

## The outcome

Luna y Junio now has its own digital sales channel, turning a scattered process into an orderly buying experience and a centralised operation.

The brand can focus on selecting garments, building its community and preparing new launches while the platform organises demand, reservations and the progress of every sale.
